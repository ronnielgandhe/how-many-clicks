# llm findings (90 wikispeedia pairs, 30 per difficulty, 11 model configs, 990 games, 2026-11-22)

- i ran 11 llm setups from anthropic, openai and google on the same 90 test pairs the lab uses: titles only, one click at a time, no back button, 15 clicks max. total api bill was $10.91.
- top of the table is gemini 4 carbon at 95.6% solved (95% ci 91 to 99) for $0.0072 per solve. gpt-6.2 astra and sonnet 5.5 with thinking tie at 94.4%. honestly the top seven are all between 92% and 96% with overlapping cis, so at n=90 i can't call a real winner.
- the cheap end is the story: gpt-6 luna solves 83.3% for $0.00039 per solve and haiku 5.5 solves 87.8% for $0.0015. opus 5.5 gets 93.3% at $0.035 per solve, so about 90x luna's cost per solve for 10 more points.
- hard pairs (4+ clicks optimal) are where tiers split: carbon 90%, opus/astra/gemini 3.8 flash 86.7%, haiku 73.3%, luna 70%, flash-lite 63.3%. easy is basically solved by everyone (90% to 100%).
- my little model (533k params, 625 KB, runs in the browser) hits 82.2% on the full 450-pair set: 98% easy, 86% medium, 62.7% hard, with a 200-page search budget and backtracking. that's a wash with the cheapest llms (luna 83.3%, flash-lite 84.4%) and 10 to 13 points behind the mid and flagship tiers, mostly on hard (62.7% vs 80% to 90%).
- where my model wins: paths and price. its solved paths are 0.75 clicks over optimal vs 1.1 to 1.7 for every llm, and it costs $0 per game. heads up that its numbers are on the full 450 pairs, not recomputed on my 90 (per-pair results aren't saved), and it's search with a back button, not a walk.
- for scale, real wikispeedia players on the 38 of my pairs people actually played finished 96.5% of games at 1.43 clicks over optimal (median path). the best llms land right around people on success.
- llms basically never lose by making stuff up. 85 of 86 failed games ran out of clicks after revisiting a page, usually about 4 visits to the same page. loops are the failure mode, same as llm-wikirace found.
- failures are mostly a few pairs, not random bad luck: 5 of the 90 pairs cause 60.5% of all failures. their targets have 1 to 6 inbound links in the whole graph vs a median of 17.5.
- clearest example is rabat to war in somalia: every model heads straight for somalia and circles it, but the only page that links to the target is nato. they go to the page that sounds closest, not the page that links in. that's a semantic trap more than hub blindness.
- invalid links are rare and only show up at the bottom: luna 1.3% of calls, flash-lite 1.7%, everyone else zero. one game total was lost to it (flash-lite asked for australia from united kingdom twice).
- the "planning gap" from the paper barely shows up here: sonnet with xhigh thinking 94.4% vs 93.3% without, gpt-6.2 sol on high 92.2% vs 92.1% on low. reasoning trims path length (1.61 to 1.46 and 1.34 to 1.12 clicks over optimal) for 18% to 33% more cost per game.
- this isn't the llm-wikirace setup: their graph is 549k pages with 7 to 8 click hard paths and links pre-cut to the 50 closest; mine is the 4,604-page wikispeedia graph, optimal paths of 1 to 6, and the full link list every step with no filtering. their best hard score is 29%; my "hard" is a much shorter game, so don't compare the two numbers.
- next i'd run my model as a pure walker (one click, no backtracking, 15 max) on these exact 90 pairs so it's a real head to head, then give the llms a back button and see if loops stop being fatal, then try the low-inbound-link targets on their own as a trap set.
